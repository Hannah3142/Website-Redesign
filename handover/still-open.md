## Standing rules (already in the preview, still true later)

These are easy to undo by accident in WordPress, so they stay written down.

- Always write **Te Wāhi Ora** with the macron. The logo has no macron; do not “fix” the logo.
- The monthly gathering is called **Check In**, never Check Up.
- You **may** say it is not a clinical service, and that it is not staffed by medical professionals. Use the house’s own line: the fence at the top of the cliff, rather than the ambulance at the bottom. You **must not** say “not a crisis service” — Jordan was clear that this could turn away women who still belong here.
- Hire of the house is an **enquiry**, not a self-serve product. Staff decide whether the use fits (women’s rest, healing or learning — not a party house).
- Gift vouchers belong with **Support**. The cookbook belongs with **Nourishment**. The footer should not grow extra shop links for those.
- Keep regular WordPress updates simple. After launch, a media manager (not a developer) should be able to change copy, photos, and events.

The tone Jordan asked for: calm, restorative, safe, welcoming. Rest, slow down, reconnect, support mental and emotional wellbeing. A charitable retreat run by women for women — “Creating Space for Women.” Restoration rather than a clinical or medical model. Rest, nourishment, nature, connection, nervous system. Check In as supportive time among other women. Accessibility, including funded or subsidised stays. Women’s Healing Sanctuary (Australia) was mentioned only as inspiration for **structure and ease of use**, not for their history, character, or purpose.

## Booking and payment (draft only)

Hannah wrote a booking and payment idea. It is **not** professional advice, and it is **not** decided. Please ask **Harper Digital** before anyone chooses a tool. There is a clickable diagram of the same idea in [booking-proposal/index.html](booking-proposal/index.html). Figures were ballparks for September 2026.

**The idea in plain language**

Today, a woman mostly fills a WordPress form or emails bookings. Staff check a diary and reply. Payment is bank transfer (the live site still says they do not take cards). Check In has its own RSVP. If she also wants a bed, she emails bookings as well. Paid events are a page plus contact — not a ticket shop.

The draft splits two kinds of booking:

1. **The programme** (a Check In day, or a themed weekend) could sit in **Humanitix** at the charity rate, embedded on the website. Check In would be a free RSVP, so nobody pays a fee. A paid event ticket would have a small Humanitix fee, paid by the guest, so the Trust still receives the listed ticket price. Humanitix would **not** sell beds.
2. **Every bed** (an ordinary rest stay, a night around Check In, or a night around an event) would go through **one stay system**, at the same published rates — from $180 a night including meals, or $60 for a day stay.

**Hire** would stay an enquiry. If it fits, they pay by bank transfer. **Subsidies** would still be decided by a human, never taken as an online checkout.

When someone pays for a stay or a paid ticket, the draft offers two paths: **pay online** (card or Humanitix), with the **guest covering the platform fee**, or **bank transfer**, which has no extra fee for anyone. Staff still mark bank transfers as paid.

**Stay option A vs stay option B**

- **A — form and emails.** The Trust does not pay for stay-booking software. The guest fills a form, waits for emails, then pays. Staff still run a diary by hand, so two people can still ask for the same nights. Work for ordinary beds is about the same as now. What you buy is a more professional front (cards, a clearer form), not a lighter week.
- **B — a live calendar.** The Trust **pays** monthly or yearly for software. The guest sees which nights are free and books on the site. The calendar blocks nights itself, so diary work on ordinary stays actually goes down. Bank transfers still need someone to mark them paid.

Humanitix, in both versions, is a new login. Paid events would take more setup than a contact page, in return for a proper attendee and payment list. A woman who wants the programme **and** a bed would still book twice — that is already how Check In works today.

**Whether switching is worth it**

It is worth switching if they want the site to take money and RSVPs the way a current retreat site does, or if they will pay for option B to stop chasing the diary. It is **not** worth switching if the only aim is less admin and they would pick A — that is the same job with extra software.

**Still to decide**

- Stay A or stay B.
- How long to hold an unpaid bank-transfer booking (for example five days), so a bed is not blocked forever.
- Budget, and Harper’s view of the tools.
